What Is NEPQ?

NEPQ stands for Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning. It is Jeremy Miner's complete reversal of old-school selling. Instead of pitching, convincing, and handling objections — you ask calm, curious questions that let the prospect persuade himself. The prospect does 80% of the talking. You do 80% of the listening. And he talks himself into the sale.

"The person asking the questions is always the one in control of the conversation. Not the person talking the most." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The Core Idea In One Sentence

🎯
The Whole Method

People are not persuaded by what WE say. They are only persuaded by what THEY say. So your entire job is to ask the questions that get the shop owner to say — out loud, in his own words — that he has a problem, that it's costing him money, and that he wants it fixed. When he says it, he believes it. When you say it, he resists it.

The Old Way vs The NEPQ Way

Old-School Selling (Dead)

Talk 80% of the time. Pitch features. Build "value." Overcome objections by pushing back. Create pressure. "Convince" the prospect. Sound excited and high-energy. The prospect feels sold TO — and his guard goes up. Every word you say, he mentally argues against.

The NEPQ Way (Works)

Listen 80% of the time. Ask curious questions. Let the prospect discover his own pain. Let objections dissolve through questions. Zero pressure. The prospect "convinces" himself. Sound calm, curious, relaxed. The prospect feels understood — and his guard comes down. Every answer he gives, he believes completely.

Why Curiosity Is The Engine

The magic of NEPQ is that a genuinely curious question makes the prospect want to answer. It doesn't feel like an interrogation. It feels like someone finally cares enough to ask. When you ask, "Huh — so how's that been working for you?" with real curiosity in your tone, the shop owner leans in. He wants to explain. He wants to be part of the conversation. That is the entire game.

Why NEPQ Works On The Brain

NEPQ is not a trick. It works because of how the human brain is wired. Understand the mechanism and you'll never go back to pitching.

"When you trigger someone's curiosity, they can't NOT think about the answer. Their brain is hijacked by the open loop until it gets closed." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The 4 Brain Reasons NEPQ Wins

1
Self-Persuasion Is Permanent

When a person hears an idea from someone else, the brain flags it as "external — verify before trusting." When the person says the idea themselves, the brain flags it as "mine — already true." A shop owner who says "yeah, I'm probably losing a lot of calls" believes it 100%. If YOU say it, he believes it 30% and argues the rest.

2
Curiosity Creates An Open Loop

A curious question opens a loop in the brain that demands closure. "Huh — I'm curious, how are you currently handling all the calls that come in after you close?" His brain cannot ignore that. It has to answer — even if just internally. That pull into the conversation is involuntary.

3
Lowered Defenses = Open Ears

The moment a prospect feels "sold to," his amygdala fires and logic shuts off. NEPQ never triggers that response because you're not selling — you're asking. No pitch means no threat. No threat means the guard stays down and he actually hears you.

4
Emotion Drives, Logic Justifies

People buy on emotion and justify with logic. NEPQ questions are engineered to surface the EMOTION first — the frustration of missed calls, the stress of an overwhelmed service writer, the fear of the competitor down the street. Once the emotion is active, the logic (the price, the ROI) becomes easy to accept.

The Single Biggest Mistake Reps Make

⛔
Answering Your Own Question

A rep asks a great curious question... then panics in the silence and answers it himself. "So how are you handling after-hours calls — I mean, probably voicemail, right?" NO. You just stole his self-persuasion. Ask the question. Then shut up. Let the silence do the work. The 3-second pause is where the sale happens.

Tonality & The Curiosity Tone

In NEPQ, HOW you ask matters more than WHAT you ask. The exact same question asked in a salesy tone gets a wall. Asked in a curious, neutral tone, it gets an open, honest answer. This is the skill that takes the longest to master — and matters the most.

"It's not what you say. It's how you say it. Tonality is 3 to 5 times more important than the words themselves." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The 3 Tones That Run The Whole Method

1 · The Curious Tone

Your voice goes slightly UP at the end, like you genuinely wonder about the answer. It sounds like a friend who's actually interested — not a salesperson reading a script. This is the tone for almost every question in NEPQ.

SOUNDS LIKE
"Huh... so how's that been working out for you?" (voice lifts, genuinely wondering)

2 · The Concerned Tone

Softer, slower, slightly lower. Used when he reveals a pain. It communicates "I actually care about this problem you just described." It makes him feel the weight of the problem without you having to say a word about it.

SOUNDS LIKE
"Oh wow... so those calls are just... going to voicemail?" (slow, soft, a little concerned)

3 · The Neutral Tone

Flat, calm, unattached. Used when discussing price or commitment. It communicates zero desperation. You're not excited for the sale — you're just laying out facts. This calmness is what makes you trustworthy and non-threatening.

SOUNDS LIKE
"Yeah, so most shops go with the Growth plan, it's $497 a month... [pause]" (calm, matter-of-fact, no push)

The Power Tools of NEPQ Tonality

⏸️
The 3-Second Pause

After he answers, don't respond immediately. Pause for 3 full seconds. It feels like an eternity to you. To him, it feels like you're thinking carefully about what he said — AND it pulls more words out of him. People hate silence. He'll fill it with more truth. The pause is your most powerful tool.

🔁
The Echo

Repeat his last few words back as a question. He says "we're losing a lot of weekend calls." You say "...losing a lot of weekend calls?" He'll expand: "Yeah, probably 20, 30 a weekend, all voicemail." The echo makes him elaborate without you asking a new question.

🌊
Softening Words

Start questions with softeners: "I'm curious..." · "Just out of curiosity..." · "Do you mind if I ask..." · "Help me understand..." · "I'm wondering..." These remove all pressure and make the question feel safe to answer. They signal you're exploring, not attacking.

🐢
Slow Down

Talk slower than feels natural. Speed signals nervousness and "commission breath." A slow, relaxed pace signals confidence and calm. It also gives your questions room to land. Rushed questions feel like an interrogation. Slow questions feel like a conversation.

Same Words, Different Tone — The Difference

❌ Salesy Tone

"So how are you handling your missed calls right now?" — said fast, flat, and eager. He hears: "here comes a pitch." Wall goes up. Short answer: "We're fine."

✓ Curious Tone

"I'm curious... how are you guys handling all those calls that come in when you're slammed?" — said slow, warm, genuinely wondering, then silence. He hears: "someone's interested in my world." Guard drops. Long answer: "Honestly, it's a mess during the morning rush..."

🎯
The Practice That Builds This

Record yourself asking the same question in 3 tones — salesy, neutral, curious. Play it back. You'll immediately hear the difference. Then practice the curious tone until it's your default. This is the #1 skill to drill. Words are easy. Tone is the craft.

Stage 1 · Connection (The First 60 Seconds)

🤝
Goal: Become Non-Threatening

The prospect's guard is highest at the start. Your job in Stage 1 is not to pitch — it's to sound so calm and curious that his brain doesn't classify you as "salesperson." You use Connection Questions to disarm and Situation Questions to gently start mapping his world.

Question types used: Connection Questions → Situation Questions. Covered in Sections 05 and 06.

Stage 2 · Engagement (The Heart of the Call)

🔥
Goal: Let Him Discover His Own Pain

This is where 80% of the call happens and where the sale is truly made. Through Problem Awareness, Solution Awareness, and Consequence Questions, you guide the shop owner to realize — on his own — that he has a costly problem and that fixing it matters. You never tell him. You ask until he tells you.

Question types used: Problem Awareness → Solution Awareness → Consequence Questions. Covered in Sections 07, 08, and 09.

Stage 3 · Transition (The Close That Isn't a Close)

✅
Goal: Let Him Ask For The Solution

By the time you reach Stage 3, the shop owner has talked himself into needing help. Now you use Transition and Commitment Questions to move naturally into showing the solution and booking the next step. There's no hard close — because he's already sold himself. You just make it easy to say yes.

Question types used: Transition Questions → Commitment Questions. Covered in Section 10.

Connection Questions

Stage 1 · The first questions of the call. Their only job is to lower the guard and make you sound like a curious human, not a cold caller. Zero pressure. Zero pitch.

"You have to disarm before you can engage. The prospect can't hear a single thing you say until he stops seeing you as a threat." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The NEPQ Opener For A Shop Owner

CONNECTION OPENER
"Hey [Name]... I was actually hoping you could help me out for a second. [pause] I'm not totally sure if this is even a fit for your shop — but do you mind if I ask you a couple quick questions about how you guys handle your calls? And if it's not a fit, no worries at all, I'll let you go."
WHY THIS WORKS
"Hoping you could help me out" flips the power dynamic — now HE feels in control. "Not sure if it's a fit" removes all pressure. "I'll let you go" gives him an exit, which paradoxically makes him stay. This is pure NEPQ disarming.

The Rules of Connection Questions

1
Ask For Help

"I was hoping you could help me..." People are wired to help when asked directly and humbly. It lowers the guard instantly because helpers aren't targets.

2
Take The Pressure Off

"Not sure if this is even a fit..." — when you remove the assumption that you WANT the sale, he stops defending against being sold. He relaxes.

3
Give Him An Out

"If it's not a fit, I'll let you go." A prospect who feels trapped fights. A prospect who feels free to leave... stays and listens.

4
Stay Calm And Slow

Connection is destroyed by eagerness. Slow down. Sound relaxed. You have all the time in the world. That calm is contagious.

More Connection Question Options

OPTION A
"Hey [Name] — quick one, and totally fine if now's not a good time... I've been talking to a few shops in [city] and wanted to ask you something. Got a sec?"
OPTION B
"Hey [Name]... I'll be honest, this is a bit of a cold call — but I promise I'm not going to pitch you anything. I actually just had a couple questions about how your shop runs its phones. That cool?"
OPTION C
"Hi [Name] — do you mind helping me understand something real quick? I'm curious how a shop your size handles the calls that come in when everybody's already slammed."
OPTION D
"Hey [Name] — random question, and no worries if it's not relevant... when you guys get busy, who ends up catching the phones?"
⛔
Never Do This In Connection

Never say "How are you today?" (screams cold call). Never say your company name first (triggers the wall). Never sound excited (signals commission). Never launch into what you do. The Connection stage is about HIM, not you. Not one word about your product yet.

Situation Questions

Stage 1 → 2 · Gentle, low-pressure questions that map the shop owner's current world. These feel easy to answer because they're just about facts. But every answer is quietly setting up the pain you'll surface next.

"Situation questions gather the information you need — but more importantly, they get the prospect comfortable talking to you. Every answer is a small yes." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The Auto Repair Situation Questions

Q1 · The Setup
"So just so I understand your shop a little better — how many bays are you running over there?"
Q2 · The Volume
"Got it... and on a typical busy day, how many calls would you say come through? Just a ballpark."
Q3 · The Staffing
"And right now — who's the one actually picking up the phone? You got a dedicated front desk person, or is your service writer kind of doing it all?"
Q4 · The Hours
"What are your hours? And I'm curious — what happens to calls that come in after you close, or on the weekend?"
Q5 · The System
"Are you using any kind of system to catch or track those calls right now, or is it mostly just... whoever's free grabs it?"

The Secret Of Situation Questions

🔑
Every Answer Is A Setup

When he says "my service writer does it all" — you've just planted the seed for the morning-rush pain. When he says "after hours goes to voicemail" — you've planted the after-hours pain. You're not just gathering info. You're loading the questions you'll fire in Stage 2. He's handing you the ammunition.

📝
Write Down His Words

As he answers, note his EXACT words and numbers. "35 calls a day." "Service writer does everything." "$550 average ticket." You will feed these exact numbers back to him in the Consequence stage. Using his own words and numbers is what makes the pain undeniable.

⏸️
Don't Rush To The Pain

Reps get excited and jump to problem questions too fast. Stay in Situation until he's comfortable and talking freely. The more small answers he gives you, the more momentum you build. Each easy answer makes the next, harder answer feel natural.

The Bridge From Situation To Problem

Once you've mapped his world, you bridge into the pain with a soft, curious transition:

THE BRIDGE
"Okay, that makes sense... So can I ask you — when it gets really busy, like that morning rush, and your service writer's already got a customer at the counter and the phone's ringing... [pause] what usually happens to that call?"
NOTE
Notice — you didn't tell him he's missing calls. You asked what happens. He's about to tell YOU he's missing calls. That's the whole method in one move.
Problem Awareness Questions

Stage 2 · The heart of NEPQ. These questions make the shop owner NOTICE a problem he's been living with but never really examined. You're holding up a mirror — and letting him see the leak for himself.

"You never tell someone they have a problem. You ask questions that help them discover it on their own. Discovered pain sells. Assigned pain gets resisted." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The Problem Awareness Questions For Auto Repair

Q1 · The Morning Rush
"When 3 calls come in at the same time during that morning rush... how do you feel that's being handled right now? Like, honestly?"
Q2 · The Callback Reality
"And those folks who get sent to voicemail — I'm curious, in your experience, do most of them call back later? Or do they kind of just... move on?"
Q3 · The Awareness Gap
"Do you actually have a way to know how many calls you're missing? Or is it more of a... you know it's happening but you can't really see it kind of thing?"
Q4 · The Weekend Hole
"On the weekends when you're closed — those calls coming in, do you have any idea roughly how many that is? And where do they end up?"
Q5 · The No-Show Sting
"How's the no-show situation been for you? Like when someone books and just doesn't show up — how often is that happening, and what's that do to your day?"

How To Ask These The Right Way

🎭
Curious, Never Accusatory

The difference between "So you're just letting calls go to voicemail?" (accusatory — he defends) and "I'm curious, where do those calls end up when everyone's slammed?" (curious — he explains) is everything. Same information. Opposite reaction. Always choose curious.

⏸️
Then The Pause

After each problem question — silence. Let him sit with it. This is uncomfortable but essential. In that pause, he's realizing something he's been avoiding. Don't rescue him from that realization. The discomfort IS the awareness forming.

🔁
Echo And Deepen

When he says "yeah, they probably don't call back" — echo it: "...they don't call back." [pause] Then deepen: "So what do you think that's costing you, roughly?" You're layering awareness on awareness. Each echo makes the problem more real to him.

What You're Listening For

You know Problem Awareness is working when the shop owner starts saying things like:

"Yeah, honestly it's probably worse than I think."
"I know we're losing calls, I just don't know how many."
"The weekends are definitely a problem."
"No-shows are killing me, actually."
🎯
The Shift

When he goes from "we're fine" at the start of the call to "yeah, it's probably worse than I think" — you've done your job. He's now AWARE. He owns the problem. And you never had to tell him a thing. Now he's ready for the questions that make him feel the cost.

Solution Awareness Questions

Stage 2 · Now that he sees the problem, these questions make him picture what "fixed" looks like — and want it. You're getting him to describe his ideal outcome in his own words, so the solution feels like HIS idea.

"Get them to tell you what the perfect solution looks like. Then when you present, you're just handing them exactly what they described wanting." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The Solution Awareness Questions For Auto Repair

Q1 · The Ideal Picture
"So let me ask you this... if you could wave a magic wand and every single call got answered — day, night, weekend, holidays — what do you think that would do for the shop?"
Q2 · The Monday Morning
"Imagine you walk in Monday morning and there's already 15 appointments booked from the weekend, sitting on your calendar. [pause] How would that change how your week starts?"
Q3 · The Service Writer Relief
"What if your service writer never had to choose between the customer at the counter and the phone ringing — because something else was catching every call? What would that free him up to do?"
Q4 · The No-Show Fix
"And if your no-shows dropped from — what'd you say, 3 or 4 a day — down to almost none, because everyone got automatic reminders... what's that worth to you in a week?"

Why This Stage Is So Powerful

🧠
He Builds The Solution In His Own Head

When the shop owner describes what "every call answered" would do for his business, HE is building the case for buying. He's not hearing your pitch — he's making his own. By the time you show him PureCode, he's already decided he wants exactly what it does. You're just confirming it exists.

💭
Emotion Through Imagination

"Imagine you walk in Monday to 15 booked appointments" makes him FEEL the relief and excitement of that outcome before he owns it. That emotional preview is what creates desire. He now wants that feeling. And there's only one way to get it.

🔗
His Words Become Your Pitch

Whatever he says here — "it'd take a huge weight off," "I could actually focus on the cars," "we'd book way more work" — write it down. When you transition to the solution, you use HIS exact phrases: "You said it'd take a weight off your service writer — that's exactly what this does."

The Micro-Commitment Question

Before moving on, lock in a small yes that sets up everything after:

MICRO-COMMITMENT
"So it sounds like if there was a way to catch every one of those calls — without hiring another person or adding work for your service writer — that's something you'd at least want to take a look at? [pause]"
NOTE
When he says "yeah, I'd look at it" — he's given you permission to show the solution. He asked for it. Now the presentation isn't a pitch — it's a response to his request. That's the NEPQ close setting itself up.
Consequence Questions

Stage 2 · The most powerful questions in NEPQ. These make the shop owner feel the real cost of NOT fixing the problem. This is where the urgency comes from — not from you pushing, but from him realizing what inaction is costing him.

"Consequence questions are where the deal is won. When the prospect feels the pain of staying the same, change becomes the only logical option." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The Consequence Questions For Auto Repair

Q1 · The Money Math
"So if you're missing — let's say even just 5 calls a day — and your average repair order is $550 like you mentioned... [pause] have you ever added up what that's costing you over a month?"
Q2 · The Lifetime Loss
"And here's the part that gets me — that's not just one $550 job you're losing. That customer would've come back for oil changes, brakes, everything, for years. What do you think a loyal customer is worth to you over their lifetime?"
Q3 · The Competitor Reality
"When that customer can't reach you and calls the shop down the road instead... and they answer... [pause] whose customer is that now, going forward?"
Q4 · The Time Cost
"If this keeps going the way it's going — same as the last year — where does that leave you 12 months from now? Is that a direction you're okay with?"
Q5 · The Emotional Cost
"How does it sit with you knowing that every day, there's customers trying to give you money and just... not getting through?"

The Rules of Consequence Questions

⚡
This Is Where Urgency Is Born

Old-school reps manufacture fake urgency ("this deal expires today!"). NEPQ creates REAL urgency by helping the prospect feel the ongoing cost of doing nothing. He's not rushing because you pushed. He's moving because he finally sees the bleeding won't stop on its own.

💰
Let HIM Do The Math

Don't say "you're losing $16,000 a month." Ask "have you ever added up what that costs?" and let him calculate — or calculate WITH him, guiding gently. When the number comes out of his mouth, it's real. When it comes out of yours, it's a sales tactic.

🎭
Stay Gentle, Not Aggressive

These questions are heavy — so your tone must be soft and concerned, never gloating or pushy. You're a friend pointing out something painful because you care, not a salesman twisting the knife. The concern in your voice is what makes the hard question land without triggering defense.

⏸️
The Longest Pause Goes Here

After "where does that leave you 12 months from now?" — say NOTHING. Let it hang. This is the most important silence in the entire call. He's staring down the barrel of another year of losses. Let him sit in that. Then he'll be the one to say "yeah, I need to fix this."

The Transition That Follows Consequence

Once he's felt the cost, you don't pounce. You ask permission with total calm:

THE CALM TRANSITION
"Yeah... [pause] So, would it be helpful if I showed you how a few other shops in [city] are catching all those calls now? It might be a fit, it might not — but at least you'd see how it works."
WHY
After all that consequence, he WANTS the solution. But you stay neutral — "might be a fit, might not." That calm detachment makes you more trustworthy and makes him want it more. You're not desperate. You're just offering to show him.
Transition & Commitment Questions

Stage 3 · The prospect has sold himself. Now you move smoothly into the solution and let him commit to the next step. In NEPQ, there is no hard close — there's a natural conclusion the prospect reaches on his own.

"If you've asked the right questions, the close is just a formality. The prospect closes himself. You just hold the door open." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

Presenting The Solution The NEPQ Way

🎯
Present In His Words, Not Yours

When you show PureCode, tie every feature back to something HE said. "You mentioned the weekends are a black hole — so here's what handles that. You said your service writer's drowning in the morning — this is what catches the overflow." You're not pitching features. You're solving the exact problems he described.

THE SOLUTION BRIDGE
"So based on everything you just told me — the morning rush, the weekend calls, the no-shows — here's what we'd set up for you. A 24/7 AI receptionist, trained on your shop, that answers every call, books appointments straight into your calendar, texts back anyone who doesn't connect, and sends reminders so the no-shows basically disappear. [pause] Everything you said was a problem — this handles it."

Transition Questions

T1 · Check Understanding
"Does that make sense so far? Or is there any part of that you'd want me to walk through again?"
T2 · Gauge Fit
"How does that line up with what you were picturing when I asked about the magic wand earlier?"
T3 · Surface Concerns
"I'm curious — what's your honest first reaction to all this?"

Commitment Questions

These get him to commit to the next step in his own words — no pressure, no hard close.

C1 · The Natural Close
"So where do you feel like we should go from here?"
NOTE
This is the most powerful close in NEPQ. You ask HIM where to go next. If you've built the pain and shown the fit, he'll say "well, how do we get started?" — and he just closed himself.
C2 · The Soft Assumption
"The way it usually works is we do a quick 30-minute setup call, get you live in about 10 days. Would something early next week work, or is later in the week better for you?"
C3 · The Value Confirm
"Just so I'm clear on my end — from what you've seen, is this something you feel would actually help the shop? [pause] ...Yeah? What makes you say that?"
WHY C3 IS GENIUS
"What makes you say that?" gets him to list the reasons he wants it — out loud, to himself. He's now verbally reinforcing his own decision. He talks himself past his own hesitation. This is self-persuasion closing the deal.

When He Hesitates — The NEPQ Response

🌊
Never Push. Ask.

If he says "let me think about it," you don't push back. You get curious: "Totally fair — I'm curious, what specifically would you want to think through?" This surfaces the REAL concern (which is never "thinking") so you can ask questions around it. You dissolve hesitation with curiosity, never with pressure.

Objections · The NEPQ Way

In old-school selling, you "overcome" objections by pushing back. In NEPQ, you dissolve them with questions. You never argue. You get curious about the objection until the prospect talks himself out of it.

"Never answer an objection with a statement. Answer it with a question. The moment you push back, you're in a fight. The moment you get curious, you're on the same side." — Jeremy Miner · NEPQ

The NEPQ Objection Formula

— He Says —
"It's too expensive."
— You Ask (calm, curious) —
"Yeah, I hear you... I'm curious though — when you say expensive, is it that the price itself feels high, or is it more that you're not yet sure it'd pay for itself? [pause]"

Then, whatever he says, go deeper: "Gotcha. So if you knew for a fact it'd bring in more than it costs — would the price still feel like the issue?" He'll say no. Now it's about value, not price — and you've already built the value.
— He Says —
"I need to think about it."
— You Ask —
"Totally fair... I'm curious, when you say think about it — what specifically are you wanting to think through? Because sometimes it's the price, sometimes it's whether it'll actually work, sometimes it's just timing. [pause] What is it for you?"

This surfaces the REAL objection hiding behind "think about it." Then you ask questions around that real thing.
— He Says —
"My service writer handles the calls."
— You Ask —
"That's good he's on it... help me understand though — when he's got a customer standing right in front of him AND two lines ringing, which one does he pick? [pause] ...And where do the other two go?"

You didn't argue. You asked a question that makes him see the gap himself.
— He Says —
"We're doing fine without it."
— You Ask —
"Yeah, sounds like the shop's doing well... I'm just curious — 'fine' compared to what? Like, do you actually know how many calls slip through, or is it more of a feeling that things are okay? [pause]"

Gently challenges "fine" by asking if it's measured or assumed. Usually it's assumed — and now he's curious too.
— He Says —
"I've tried something like this before and it didn't work."
— You Ask —
"Oh interesting — what happened with the last one? [pause, listen] ...Yeah, that would frustrate me too. So what would need to be different this time for you to feel good about trying again?"

You validate, get the full story, then let HIM define what a good solution looks like — which you then match.
— He Says —
"Just send me some info."
— You Ask —
"Happy to... I'm curious though, when you say send info — what exactly would you be looking for in it? Because I could send a generic brochure, but that probably won't tell you what you actually want to know. What's the real question you'd want answered? [pause]"

"Send info" is usually a soft brush-off. This turns it into a real conversation about his actual concern.
— He Says —
"I don't have time for this right now."
— You Ask —
"Totally get it, you're slammed — and honestly, that's kind of the whole point, right? [pause] If you're too busy to catch every call, I'm curious... where do you think those calls are going while you're heads-down? [pause]"

You reframe his objection AS the problem, gently, with a question. His lack of time IS the pain.
— He Says —
"I need to talk to my partner."
— You Ask —
"Makes sense, that's a smart way to run it... I'm curious, what do you think your partner's biggest question is going to be when you bring it to them? [pause] ...Let's make sure you've got a solid answer for that before you talk to them."

You help him prepare to sell it internally — and surface any hidden objections he's holding.

The Golden Rule Of NEPQ Objections

The prospect is never wrong, and you never argue. You just get genuinely curious about WHY he feels that way — and keep asking soft questions until the objection either dissolves or reveals the real concern underneath. Curiosity is the solvent that dissolves every wall. Pressure is the glue that makes them harder.

Here is a complete NEPQ call for an auto repair shop, from the first hello to the booked appointment — every question in sequence. This is your master script. Internalize the flow, not the exact words.

YOU · CONNECTION

"Honestly? It probably goes to voicemail. He can't do everything."

"Yeah... [pause] And those voicemail folks — in your experience, do most of them call back? Or do they kinda just move on to the next shop?"
HIM
"...Probably move on, yeah. I don't get a ton of voicemails."

"So here's what I'm curious about — if even 5 of those a day are real appointment calls, and your average repair order is what, 500-ish?"

"About $550, yeah."

Answers every call, books straight into your calendar, texts back anyone who doesn't connect, and sends reminders so no-shows drop off. Everything you just said was a problem — this handles it."

You never said "our product is great." You never listed features to convince him. You never fought an objection. You asked. He answered. He realized. He decided. Every important conclusion came out of HIS mouth. That's why it works — and why it doesn't feel like being sold.

Drills & Practice

NEPQ is a skill, and skills are built through reps. These drills train the two hardest parts: asking with genuine curiosity, and staying silent after you ask.

Drill 1 · The Tonality Recorder

🎙️
Record The Same Question 3 Ways

Take one question — "how are you handling those calls right now?" Record it three times: once salesy, once flat/neutral, once genuinely curious. Play them back. Train your ear to hear the difference, then drill the curious version until it's automatic. Do this daily with a new question each time.

Drill 2 · The Silence Challenge

⏸️
Ask, Then Count To 3

The hardest NEPQ skill is shutting up after a question. Partner up. Ask a question, then silently count "one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand, three-one-thousand" before you're allowed to speak. It'll feel unbearable at first. That discomfort is exactly what you're training through. The pause pulls out gold.

Drill 3 · No Statements Allowed

❓
Roleplay Where You Can ONLY Ask Questions

Run a full mock call where you are physically not allowed to make a single statement — only questions. It forces you out of pitch mode and into question mode. If you catch yourself about to explain something, turn it into a question instead. This rewires your instinct.

Drill 4 · The Echo Drill

🔁
Repeat Their Last 3 Words

Partner says something. You respond only by echoing their last few words as a question, then pausing. "...going to voicemail?" [pause] Watch how much more they say. Train this until echoing becomes reflexive — it's the easiest way to keep them talking without thinking of a new question.

Drill 5 · Objection Curiosity

🌊
Answer Every Objection With A Question

Have a partner throw objections at you rapid-fire. Your only rule: you must respond to each one with a curious question, never a statement, never a defense. "Too expensive" → "What makes you say that?" Drill until your reflex to any objection is a question, not an argument.

The 5 Signs You're Doing NEPQ Right

✓ The prospect is talking more than you

If you're talking more than 20-30% of the call, you've slipped back into pitching.

✓ He's telling YOU his problems

You never had to inform him of anything. He's discovering it out loud.

✓ There are comfortable silences

The pauses don't feel awkward — they feel like thinking. That's trust.

✓ He asks "so how does this work?"

When HE asks for the solution, you've done it right. He's pulling, not being pushed.

✓ It didn't feel like selling

The best NEPQ calls feel like two people having an honest conversation. Because they are.

✓ He closes himself

"How do we get started?" comes from him. You just answer it.

Don't pitch. Ask.
Don't convince. Get curious.
Don't push. Pause.
Let him persuade himself — and he'll thank you for it.
