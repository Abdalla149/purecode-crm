1 The Opener
Goal: survive the first 20 seconds. No pitch. Ask a question. Then stop talking.
▸ Best opener — the numbers hook
"Hey [Name] — quick question, I'll be 20 seconds. The average shop your size is losing around $11,000 a month just from calls that hit voicemail. Do you guys actually track how many calls you miss during the day?"
▸ Backup — the after-hours angle
"Hey [Name] — when you guys close at 5, what happens to the calls that come in after that? Voicemail, or do you have something catching them?"
▸ Backup — the peer / curiosity angle
"Hey [Name], I was hoping you could help me out for a sec. I'm not gonna pitch you — I just had a couple questions about how your shop handles its calls. That cool?"
2 Discovery Questions
Ask these in order. Write down his numbers — you'll use them in the math.
▸ Setup
"How many bays are you running over there?"
▸ Volume
"On a busy day, roughly how many calls come through? Ballpark."
▸ Staffing
"Who's grabbing the phone right now — a front desk person, or is your service writer doing it all?"
▸ Hours
"What happens to calls after you close, or on weekends?"
▸ Average ticket
"What's your average repair order run — ballpark?"
3 Pain Questions
Make HIM say it's a problem. Ask, then pause 3 seconds. Don't rescue the silence.
▸ The morning rush
"When 3 calls come in at once during the morning rush and your guy's already with a customer — [pause] what happens to the other 2?"
▸ The callback reality
"Those folks who hit voicemail — do most of them call back? Or do they just call the next shop?"
▸ The awareness gap
"Do you actually have a way to know how many calls you're missing? Or is it more of a 'you know it's happening but can't see it' thing?"
▸ The no-show sting
"How's the no-show situation? When someone books and doesn't show — how often, and what's it do to your day?"
▸ The cost realization
"Have you ever added up what those missed calls cost you over a month? [pause — let him do the math]"

5 The Pitch (In His Words)
Only after he's felt the pain. Tie every feature to something he told you.
▸ The bridge
"So based on what you told me — the morning rush, the weekend calls, the no-shows — here's what we set up: a 24/7 AI receptionist trained on your shop. Answers every call, books straight into your calendar, texts back anyone who doesn't connect, and sends reminders so no-shows basically disappear. Your service writer keeps doing what he does best — we just make sure the phone never goes unanswered."
▸ The proof line
"We're working with a bunch of shops already, and the average one recovers $8,000 to $16,000 a month in the first 30 days."

7 Closing
Assume the close. Ask for a date, not a "yes."
▸ The assumptive close
"If we kick this off this week, you'll be live in about 10 days. Does Tuesday or Thursday work better for the 30-minute setup call?"
▸ The NEPQ natural close
"So where do you feel like we should go from here?" [then stay silent]
▸ The risk-reversal close
"Start with Growth, month to month, no contract. 30-day money-back on the setup. If it doesn't perform, you cancel and you've lost nothing. What's the downside?"
▸ After he says yes — STOP SELLING
"Awesome. Let me get you booked for the setup call. You'll hear from our onboarding team within 24 hours. Welcome aboard."
